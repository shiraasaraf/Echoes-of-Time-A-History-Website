1. Air France Flight 139 – The Hijacked Aircraft

Air France Flight 139 was operated by an Airbus A300, a wide-body passenger aircraft. On June 27, 1976, the flight was hijacked after departing Athens on its journey from Tel Aviv to Paris. The aircraft became a central part of the hostage crisis, carrying passengers and crew who were eventually taken to Entebbe Airport in Uganda.

2. The C-130 Hercules – The Rescue Aircraft

The Israeli rescue forces traveled to Uganda aboard Lockheed C-130 Hercules military transport aircraft. These planes were capable of carrying soldiers, vehicles and equipment over long distances. Their large cargo compartments and ability to operate on relatively short runways made them essential to the mission. The aircraft also transported the rescued hostages away from Entebbe.

3. The Black Mercedes and Military Vehicles

One of the operation's most unusual details was the use of a black Mercedes-Benz and accompanying Land Rover vehicles. The rescue team used these vehicles to imitate the appearance of President Idi Amin's motorcade. This deception was intended to help the soldiers approach the old terminal before the guards realized what was happening.

4. The Old Terminal at Entebbe Airport

The old terminal building at Entebbe Airport was where the remaining hostages were held. Its layout, entrances and surrounding roads were crucial details for the rescue planners. Israeli intelligence gathered information about the building, including details from people familiar with the airport. This knowledge helped the forces prepare for the assault and locate the hostages quickly.

Why was a black Mercedes used?

Why were Hercules aircraft important?

The C-130 Hercules aircraft could transport soldiers, vehicles and equipment over long distances, making the rescue mission possible.
